Five layout changes to make billing easier to read

Everything below is presentation only. No wording, no logic, no data model, no new states — the same fields in the same order, laid out so a caregiver can read one column top to bottom instead of scanning left and right. Each section shows the screen as it is today next to the proposed version.

Layout only No copy changes No new components Desktop ≥ 860px is where all of this shows

The original prototype with every change below applied — click through it the same way you would the one you sent.

1

Work order — one column, one price block

The form currently splits into two reading columns twice, the price block draws six horizontal lines around three numbers, and the one field for flagging a problem is hidden behind a link.

What is wrong now

  • Hours worked and What you did sit side by side, and the two fields are wildly different widths — 84px next to a full-width input.
  • Eating and Moving around are forced into two columns at ≥860px. Each column is then too narrow for its three chips, so More than usual wraps onto a second line in both.
  • The price block has a dotted leader on every row, a solid rule above the total, and another rule above each note — six lines for three numbers.
  • The total You receive is the same size as the rows above it, so nothing carries the eye to the number that matters.
  • Something worried me today is a link that has to be clicked before the field appears. A caregiver skimming the form has no idea it is there, and nothing says whether it is expected of them.

What changes

  • One column throughout. Every label sits above its own field, full width, stacked. Nothing pairs.
  • Eating goes above Moving around. At full modal width all three chips fit on one line, so no chip wraps.
  • Drop every dotted leader. Amounts right-align on their own; the gap already reads as a pair.
  • Keep exactly one divider — above You receive. The notes underneath lose their rules and sit as plain text.
  • The total amount goes 13px → 19px and its label to 14px, so the number carries and the words stay quiet. Everything else in the block stays 13.5px, and the fee row darkens slightly — it is a real deduction, not disabled text.
  • Something worried you today? becomes a field that is always visible, marked OPTIONAL, with one line saying when to use it and where it goes. No click to discover it, and no pressure to fill it.

Plan the next visit — controls that match, timing out of the price

Three controls sit in one row and all three are a different height. The line that tells you when the visit runs is buried at the top of the price block, where it has nothing to do with money.

What is wrong now

  • 3 h · as agreed is built from a 15px bold number plus 12px caption inside 8/12 padding, so it is visibly taller and heavier than the Today / Tomorrow chips and the time input beside it. The one control you cannot change looks like the most important one.
  • The three controls have three different widths, driven by content rather than by a grid.
  • tomorrow, 09:00 – 12:00 · 25 h from now is the first line inside the price block. It describes the schedule, not the charge.
  • Same dotted leaders and stacked rules as the work order.

What changes

  • One 3-column grid. Every control is 36px tall, 13.5px, radius 9 — the day segment, the time field and the locked hours field are the same object at the same size.
  • 3 h keeps its muted as agreed caption but drops to the shared type size, and the field keeps the soft fill so it still reads as read-only.
  • The timing line moves directly under the controls as a quiet caption — it now sits next to what produces it.
  • The one component change in this whole set: Today / Tomorrow becomes a segmented control rather than two loose pills, so it fills its column and matches the field next to it exactly. If that is unwelcome, keep the two pills and give them the same 36px height — the size problem is fixed either way.
  • Price block is identical to the work order's: no leaders, one divider, the total amount at 19px. Two blocks, one pattern.

3

Care agreement, draft — no stepper, no frame, taller sheet

The form is fine. What surrounds it is not: a two-step progress bar for what is one form and its review, and a bordered card drawn inside a bordered dialog.

What is wrong now

  • Step 1 of 2 · draft with a progress bar sits above the title. This is one form that gets reviewed before sending, not a two-step wizard — the stepper promises a longer journey than there is.
  • The whole form is wrapped in border: 1px solid #EDE0D4; border-radius: 10px inside a dialog that already has a border and a radius. Two frames, 18px apart.
  • The sheet caps at max-height: 75vh, so the service list scrolls sooner than it needs to and the modal footer eats vertical space that is available.

What changes

  • Remove the stepper and its progress bar from both steps. The dialog title plus the footer button (Review agreement → Send to Eeva) already say where you are.
  • Remove the inner frame. The form sits directly on the dialog body, so the padding goes from 20 + 18 down to a single 20.
  • max-height: 75vh → 86vh (+15%). Roughly four more service rows visible before scrolling.
  • Everything inside the form stays exactly as it is.

4

Care agreement, review — one reading axis

This is the screen a caregiver checks before committing to a rate. Right now the eye has to cross the dialog five times to read it. It should read straight down.

What is wrong now

  • Five vertical axes in one short panel: labels at the left edge, big numbers flush right, category headings back at the left, then service bullets in two columns. Every row restarts the scan.
  • The two numbers that matter — €32.00/h and 3 h — are split across two rows and pushed to the far right, away from the words that explain them.
  • Services are dot-bulleted here but shown as chips everywhere else in the product — the request cards, the caregiver profile, the work order. The same data gets a different shape on the one screen where it is being confirmed.
  • Four stacked border-top rules cut the panel into slabs, on top of the inner frame and the dialog border.
  • Same stepper as the draft step.

What changes

  • Everything left-aligns on one axis. Nothing is flush right.
  • Rate and hours become the headline — one line, 29px, with the derived €96.00 per visit as the caption directly beneath. That is the answer the caregiver came for, read first.
  • Services become chips, matching the rest of the product, grouped under the same small category label used in the draft step.
  • These chips are the outlined read-only variant, not the filled one. Nothing here is selectable — a filled accent chip would invite a click that does nothing. The outlined chip already exists in the product, on the request cards.
  • Rules drop from four to one hairline, plus the soft lock note at the bottom which keeps its fill instead of a border.
  • Stepper and inner frame removed, as in step 1.

Earnings — the row opens the work order

Earnings lists every visit and the amount it produced, but there is no way to get from the amount to the work order that produced it. The row is the natural place to ask.

What is wrong now

  • Every visit row is a <div>. Nothing is clickable, nothing hovers, there is no chevron.
  • To see what was actually filled in for a visit, the caregiver has to leave Earnings, go to Clients, find the person, and open the visit from there.
  • The three-step tracker names Work order as a stage but gives no way to look at it.

What changes

  • The row becomes a <button> that opens the existing work order side panel — the same one the board already opens. No new screen to design.
  • Standard affordances: chevron on the right, and the hover treatment already used on the earnings summary cards (border-color: #FC703C plus the soft shadow).
  • A visit with no work order yet opens the same panel in its pre-visit state — reserved amount, hold expiry, what is still owed. Nothing is ever a dead click.
